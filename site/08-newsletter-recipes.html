Chapter 08

8. Newsletter recipes

Newsletters can be calm — but only if you archive them like a filing system, not like a friend. One daily AI newsletter is three minutes of reading; ten of them are a second doomscroll, delivered to your inbox with today’s date on it. This chapter gives you pickup recipes and the folder rules that keep email doing email’s job.

The cadence rule

At most: one daily, one weekly, one monthly. Anything beyond that competes for the same attention and low-quality duplication wins. Rotation beats accumulation: when a new newsletter interest arrives, one old one leaves. (This is the same keep/cut discipline as the quarterly refresh.)

The pickup list

Daily (pick one)

  • TLDR AI (tldr.tech) — five headlines with one-sentence summaries, linked. Built for a 3-minute skim. Best default.
  • The Neuron — a slightly chattier daily digest with a “casual/technical” tone slider. Good if you like a little voice in your news.
  • AI News by character-level aggregators (Hacker Newsletter-style digests) — pick whichever your experiment month shows you actually open.

Weekly (pick one)

  • The Batch (DeepLearning.AI) — Andrew Ng’s editorial plus five stories, written for the general technical audience. The calmest serious AI newsletter in the game.
  • Last Week in AI — longer-form weekly roundup with briefs and research summaries; better if you want coverage depth without daily volume.

Monthly / slow (pick one)

  • One Useful Thing (Ethan Mollick) — posts roughly weekly but reads monthly-scale; essays about what’s actually useful, not what just shipped.
  • Import AI (Jack Clark) — deeper, more research-and-policy flavored; pick this only if you genuinely like reading policy-adjacent analysis.

The folder recipes (make email bend to you)

Recipe 1 — The filter rule (works in Gmail, Outlook, Fastmail, Thunderbird):

  1. Create a folder/label called AI News.
  2. Make a rule: messages from the newsletters’ sender addresses (or to your subscription address) → skip the inbox, mark as read if you like, move to AI News. In Gmail: “Skip Inbox, Apply label.”
  3. Result: your inbox never sees AI news. You check the folder on your schedule — say, twice a week — and it’s already summarized by you choosing the senders.

Recipe 2 — The digest address: create a free email alias like yourname+ai@... (plus-addressing) and use it for all AI subscriptions. One rule on that address handles every current and future newsletter, no per-sender maintenance.

Recipe 3 — The Sunday clear: one weekly 10-minute appointment: open AI News, read the weekly item fully, skim the dailies’ headlines, then select all → archive. Archive is not deleting; it’s admitting you’re not reading it. The folder starts each week fresh.

Unsubscribe without guilt

  • Two consecutive skips of a newsletter = unsubscribe. You can resubscribe any time; they’ll take you back.
  • Use the built-in unsubscribe links; they work in most quality newsletters.
  • If a newsletter automated you into opening it via FOMO subject lines (“The model that changes everything …”), that’s a hype-word source in disguise — treat it per Chapter 3 and vote with unsubscription.

The combined calm stack (RSS + newsletter)

If you follow both chapters at once, here’s the minimal full setup:

  • Reader (RSS): provider changelogs + Simon Willison + your community pick (Chapter 7, Recipe A). Titles-first reading.
  • Email: TLDR AI daily + The Batch weekly, routed to a AI News folder, cleared Sundays.
  • Slot: 15 minutes twice a week is the entire budget. Timer on, folder open, mark-all-read when done.

That’s it — a complete, calm, free AI news operation: providers (primary), one practitioner (context), one daily and one weekly (digests), one folder and one reader (systems), one calendar slot (discipline). Everything else is optional expansions, and none of them cost money or your pulse.
