# 3. Hype words to discount

Certain words reliably show up in AI coverage before, during, and after entirely ordinary events. You can't stop them, but you can **lower the volume automatically** when you see them. This chapter is a decoder. None of this means the underlying news is fake — only that these words promise *more* than most announcements deliver.

## Words that mean "incremental update"

| Word/phrase | Sound like | Actually means |
|---|---|---|
| **"Breakthrough"** | A wall came down | A benchmark improved somewhere |
| **"Game-changing"** | Rules rewritten | A feature competitors also have |
| **"Way smarter"** | New model class | Score climbed a few points |
| **"Leaps and bounds"** | Step change | Steady progress since last version |
| **"Superhuman at X"** | It's better than you at X | Beats average humans on one measured task |
| **"10x"** | Order of magnitude | Some metric, some workload, sometimes |

When you see these, your reaction should be *"a version came out"* — then check the changelog (Chapter 2) for the actual sentence.

## Words that mean "marketing reframe"

- **"Agentic"** — the tool now does multiple steps instead of one. Useful! Not a species change.
- **"Inflection point / new era / everything changes"** — editorializing, not information. Nothing in these sentences can be true or false.
- **"Democratizes X"** — it got cheaper or easier. Good; still just a price cut of sorts.
- **"Disrupts X"** — the pitch deck says they'll compete in X.
- **"First-of-its-kind"** — technically true, often because nobody needed it.

## Words that mean "be careful, but stay calm"

- **"New study shows"** — one study is a data point, not a conclusion. Wait for replication or a careful read of the method section.
- **"Researchers warn"** — usually a risk *identified*, not a risk *realized*. Note it; don't doom about it.
- **"Leaked/insider says"** — unconfirmed. Live by "wait a week" here; the truth is usually less dramatic.
- **"AGI / superintelligence / ASI"** — no agreed definition, so the sentence can't inform any decision in your week. Discount to zero until precisely defined.

## The discount method

For every hype headline, apply three questions — in order:

1. **What's the dated, named fact?** (A product, a number, a change — from a changelog or announcement.)
2. **Does it touch something I use?** If not: you may file it under "interesting" and move on. Most things you read touch nothing you use.
3. **If the hype turned out completely wrong, would anything I do tomorrow change?** If no, the news has no operational meaning for you this week.

That last question is the killer. Run it on 90% of AI coverage and the coverage quietly empties out.

## Expand your anti-hype vocabulary

Practical substitutions that keep you honest and calm:

- "breakthrough" → *an update*
- "game-changing" → *a feature*
- "revolutionary" → *a product*
- "the end of X" → *someone's forecast about X*
- "scares researchers" → *a paper discussed risk scenarios*

Read the substituted version. If it still sounds interesting, it probably is.

> **The one-sentence rule:** every hype headline is *almost always* translating to one of: **"a model updated," "a company shipped a feature," "a price changed," or "a paper got shared."** From those four, pick one, then go back to your week.
