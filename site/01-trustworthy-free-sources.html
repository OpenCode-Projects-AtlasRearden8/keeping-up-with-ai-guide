Chapter 01

1. Trustworthy free sources

The mistake most people make is subscribing to everything. You don’t need volume — you need a small bench of sources that are fast enough, honest enough, and free. Here’s a short list that clears that bar. Pick five. That’s the whole stack.

Primary sources (where news actually comes from)

These announce things first, in their own words, with no hot takes attached.

  • OpenAI — Release notes & changelog: every model and product change, dated. The single most efficient place to see what shipped.
  • Anthropic — News & changelog: same deal for Claude. Their model cards are unusually readable.
  • Google DeepMind blog: research posts and product news. Skim the summaries; skip the papers unless curious.
  • Meta AI blog: Llama releases and open-weight news.
  • Mistral AI news: short posts, frequent releases, a good signal for the open-weights side.

Why trust them: they have strong incentives to be accurate about their own products, and announcements are dated and specific. Limitation: they are marketing-adjacent. Read for facts (name, date, capability), not for spin (“world’s best model”).

Honest aggregators (people who test things)

  • Simon Willison’s blog (simonwillison.net): years of daily, hands-on notes about LLM features, with receipts. Very skimmable.
  • Interconnects (Nathan Lambert): open models, release analysis, level-headed commentary on the ecosystem.
  • Hugging Face: model releases, dataset trends, and community notes. The front page is a live “what’s happening” board, especially for open-weight and local AI.
  • r/LocalLLaMA: community-tested takes on new models within hours. Kind early comments, skip the flame threads.

Why trust them: practitioners who stake reputation on being correct rather than first. Limitation: individual taste. Cross-check anything that sounds absolute.

Curated digests (one-a-day style)

  • The Batch (DeepLearning.AI, Andrew Ng): a weekly five-minute digest calibrated for the general technical reader. Calm by design.
  • TLDR AI (tldr.tech): a short daily scannable roundup with one-line summaries and links.
  • One Useful Thing (Ethan Mollick): slower, thoughtful, focused on what tools are actually useful for. Less “news,” more “how to think.”

Why trust them: editors whose job is filtering, not scrolling. Limitation: they still choose what you see. That’s fine — that’s the point.

What to skip (for calm purposes)

  • Anything with “revolution,” “everything changed,” “AGI,” “scary new paper” in the headline (see Chapter 3).
  • Accounts that post 20 times a day about AI. No person tests that much; they’re reselling urgency.
  • Same-day takes on benchmarks. Benchmarks need reading, not reacting (that’s a different guide’s job).

Your starter stack (copy this)

  1. The changelog of the one assistant you actually use (OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google).
  2. Simon Willison’s blog — real usage notes, daily.
  3. The Batch (weekly) — September-through-Friday calm summary.
  4. One community space (r/LocalLLaMA if you run local models, otherwise skip).
  5. TLDR AI (daily, 3 minutes) or One Useful Thing (weekly, 10 minutes) — your choice of pace.

Five items. Feed them into RSS or a newsletter folder (Chapters 7 and 8), and you’re done for the year.

Rule of thumb: if a source hasn’t taught you something in two weeks, cut it and swap in one candidate from this chapter’s lists. A source list is a garden, not a museum.
